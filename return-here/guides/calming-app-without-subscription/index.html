A calming app without a subscription.

What free and one-time-purchase apps can and can’t give you, and how to tell from the store page before you pay.

The short answer

They exist, in three kinds: free apps funded by donations, apps you buy once, and tools already on your phone or watch.

Which one suits you depends on whether you want a library of guided sessions or a few seconds of pause.

A disclosure before we go on: we make Return Here, a one-time-purchase app, so we are not neutral. This guide includes free options that may suit you better.

First decide: a library or a moment

A subscription pays for a steady supply of new material: courses, daily sessions, sleep stories, new teachers. If you use those most weeks, a subscription can be a fair price for them.

Many people want something smaller. The same ten seconds, a few times a day, with nothing new to keep up with. If that’s you, a subscription means renting something you could own, and paying for a library you won’t open.

Neither choice is the wrong one. They’re different products, and it helps to know which you’re shopping for before you compare prices.

Six things to check on the store page

All of this is visible before you download anything.

  1. The in-app purchases line.

    The App Store lists them under Information, and Google Play labels the listing “In-app purchases” near the install button. If an app says free and lists a yearly plan, check whether its free features are limited or whether a paid trial automatically renews.

  2. The privacy section.

    “App Privacy” on the App Store and “Data safety” on Google Play show what the developer says it collects. For an app you open at your most frazzled, less is better.

  3. Whether it needs an account.

    Screenshots and reviews usually give this away. A sign-up wall before the first breath is a cost too.

  4. Whether it works offline.

    Look for it in the description, then test in airplane mode once you’ve installed. Streaming-only apps fail on planes and in basements.

  5. How it asks for your attention.

    Streaks, badges, and daily reminders help some people. Others find that a calming app that nags stops being calming.

  6. What recent reviews say about price.

    Sort by most recent. People are quick to report it when an app they bought outright moves to a subscription.

If an app turns out not to be what its page described, both stores have a refund request process.

Free options to try first

If you want guided meditation, start here before paying anyone, including us. The descriptions below come from each organization and were checked in November 2026. Confirm on the store page, because terms change.

Medito
Run by the nonprofit Medito Foundation. It describes the app as free, with no ads, no paywall, and no account required. A full library of guided meditations, courses, and sleep content.
Healthy Minds Program
From a nonprofit affiliated with the Center for Healthy Minds at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. It says it is funded by donations, with no subscription fee. Structured lessons plus guided practice.
What you already own
Your phone’s timer, a voice memo in your own words, or the breathing exercise built into many smartwatches. Nothing to install.
